Working title: Air spaces at the rain-garden edge
Species: Juncus effusus (soft rush)
Observation site: College teaching rain garden
Observation date: 2026-03-26
Capsule: At the college teaching rain garden, soft rush remained upright in saturated soil after overnight rain. A fallen stem showed a pale, airy pith rather than a solid core. Those connected air spaces are aerenchyma: they move oxygen from exposed shoots toward tissues in oxygen-poor, waterlogged soil. The tissue does not remove flooding, but it helps living cells keep respiring when gas diffusion through wet soil is slow. This internal ventilation helps explain why soft rush occupies the basin edge where several neighboring upland species thin out.
